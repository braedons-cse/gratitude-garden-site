Privacy policy

Last updated 4 October 2026

Your journal is personal. This page says plainly what the app collects, why, who handles it for us, and how to make it all go away.

The short version

  • We store your account, your journal and your garden so they're there on every device you sign in to.
  • No ads, no advertising ID, no analytics or tracking tools, and we never sell your data.
  • Crash reports are scrubbed on your phone before they're sent, and carry no account ID.
  • Deleting an entry erases its words and photo. Deleting your account erases everything, in the app or on the web.
  • Your journal is not end-to-end encrypted. It is private to your account, but it is stored on our servers in a form we could technically read (see who can see it).

1. Who we are

Gratitude Garden is an Android app for writing down what you're grateful for and growing a small garden as you do. It is made by {{DEVELOPER_NAME}} ("we", "us"), who is responsible for your data under this policy. Questions or requests: {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.

2. What we collect and why

Your account

  • Email address: to sign you in and to identify your account if you contact us.
  • Display name: the name you choose at sign-up, shown on your garden.
  • Password: stored only as a one-way hash by our sign-in provider. We can't see it.

Your journal

  • What you write: the text of each entry.
  • Mood (optional): a rating from 1 (rough) to 5 (great), if you pick one.
  • Photo (optional): one per entry, only the photo you choose. The app re-saves it on your phone before uploading, which removes all hidden metadata, including any location.
  • When and how: the date and time of each entry, whether it was typed or dictated, and your phone's time zone name (for example "Europe/Berlin"), so "today" means your today. A time zone tells us your rough region, not where you are.

Your garden

Coins, plants and where they're planted, things you've bought, streaks and streak freezes, level and XP, and settings such as your daily entry limit. These make the game work.

Crash reports

When the app crashes, freezes, or hits an error it didn't expect, it sends a report to Sentry so we can fix it. A report contains the technical error, your phone model and Android version, the app version, and a random ID created for this installation (not linked to your account). Before anything is sent, the app removes sign-in tokens, email addresses, account and entry IDs, web addresses' query details, and any journal text that could appear in an error. Sentry is set not to store your IP address or a location derived from it. No screenshots or screen recordings are ever taken.

Sign-up statistics

We count, from the data above, how new accounts get on in their first week (for example, how many wrote again on their second day). Nothing extra is collected for this, and only we can see it.

Why we're allowed to (for people in the EU and UK)

  • Your account, journal and garden: to provide the app you asked for (contract).
  • Crash reports and sign-up statistics: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and improving it, kept as small as we can make it.
  • Mood, photos, notifications and the microphone: only when you choose to use them (consent), which you can withdraw at any time by not using them, removing them, or turning them off.

3. What we don't collect

  • No location from GPS or your network.
  • No contacts, calendar, call logs, or other apps' data.
  • No advertising ID, no ads, and no analytics or tracking tools.
  • No audio recordings (see the microphone below).
  • We never sell your data or share it with advertisers, and we don't use it to train AI models.

4. Phone permissions

  • Microphone (optional): for speaking an entry instead of typing it. The app uses your phone's speech recognition service (on most phones, Google's), which turns your voice into text. That service receives the audio under its own privacy policy; the app only receives the text, and never records or stores audio.
  • Notifications (optional): for the daily reminder you can turn on in the app. Replying from the notification saves an entry like writing one in the app.
  • Camera and photos: the app has no camera or photo-library permission. Taking a photo opens your camera app, and picking one opens Android's photo picker; the app only receives the one photo you choose.
  • Internet and start-up: to sync, and to set your reminder again after the phone restarts.

5. Who processes your data

These companies process data on our behalf, only to provide their service to us:

  • Supabase: sign-in, the database holding your journal and garden, and photo storage. Servers in the United States.
  • Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.): crash reports. Stored in the European Union (Germany).
  • Have I Been Pwned: when you create an account, the app checks whether your new password has appeared in a known data breach. Only the first 5 characters of a one-way hash of the password are sent, never the password itself, and the match happens on your phone.
  • GitHub hosts these web pages, and the account-deletion page loads its sign-in code from jsDelivr. Like any website host, they may log visitors' IP addresses.

If you live outside the United States, your account and journal are transferred to and stored in the US. Where the law requires it, these transfers rely on our providers' standard contractual clauses.

6. Who can see your data

No other user. The app has no social features: nobody else can see your garden or your journal.

As the operator, we have administrative access to the database, which means we could technically read stored entries. We don't browse them. We look at an account's data only to fix a problem you've asked us about, to keep the service secure, or when the law requires it.

We would disclose data to authorities only when legally required to, and if the app were ever transferred to someone else, this policy would go with it and you would be told first.

7. How it's protected

  • Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS).
  • The database only lets each account reach its own rows, and photos sit in private storage that only their owner's account can reach.
  • The copy of your journal on your phone is left out of Android backups and phone-to-phone transfers, and is wiped when you sign out.
  • New passwords must be at least 8 characters with upper and lower case letters, a number and a symbol, and must not appear in a known breach.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach ever affected your data, we would tell you and, where required, the authorities.

8. Keeping and deleting

  • An entry: edit or delete it any time. Deleting it erases its text, mood and photo from our servers as soon as the deletion reaches them (straight away when you're online, otherwise the next time you are). A bare record of the day it was written stays, so daily limits and streaks keep working, until you delete your account.
  • Your account: delete it in the app (Me → Delete account) or on the web without the app. Everything goes at once: your account, journal, photos, garden, coins and stats. Deleting in the app also wipes the copy on that phone; after deleting on the web, uninstall the app or log out of it to remove the phone's copy.
  • Crash reports are kept by Sentry for up to 90 days.
  • Our providers' backups and logs may hold copies for a short time after deletion before they're overwritten.

We keep your account for as long as you have it. We don't delete inactive accounts.

9. Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, or to delete it, and you can object to how we use it. Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU and UK, or the CCPA in California) you have these rights by law, but we offer them to everyone. Email {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} from your account's email address and we'll reply within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

10. Children

Gratitude Garden isn't meant for children under 13 (or the minimum age for this kind of service where you live). We don't knowingly collect their data; if you think a child has an account, tell us and we'll delete it.
